# Acquired Immunity One Shot Note

## Concept in Simple Words

Acquired immunity (or adaptive immunity) is the highly specialized defense system that you build up throughout your life as you encounter different germs. Unlike the general shields you are born with, this system acts like a smart military network: it creates custom-targeted weapons (antibodies) against specific invaders and files away their profiles (memory cells) so it can wipe them out instantly if they ever dare to return.

## Why This Topic Matters in CEE

CEE exams heavily target the exact functional and structural differences between B-cells and T-cells, active versus passive immunity, and the structural components of an antibody molecule. Questions regularly focus on the specific immunoglobulins ($IgG$, $IgA$, $IgM$, $IgE$, $IgD$), their locations, placenta-crossing abilities, and the primary versus secondary immune responses.

## Must Remember Points

* **Four Core Features:** Acquired immunity is characterized by four distinct traits: **Specificity** (distinguishing minute differences between pathogens), **Diversity** (recognizing billions of distinct antigens), **Discrimination between self and non-self**, and **Memory**.
* **Primary Immune Response:** The initial, low-intensity response elicited when the body encounters a pathogen for the very first time. It has a long lag phase and is dominated primarily by **$IgM$** antibodies.
* **Secondary (Anamnestic) Response:** A highly intensified, rapid, and massive response generated upon subsequent encounters with the exact same pathogen. It is powered by **Memory cells** and relies heavily on **$IgG$** antibodies.
* **B-Lymphocytes (B-Cells):** Generated and matured in the **bone marrow**. They produce an army of proteins called **antibodies** into body fluids to fight foreign invaders.
* **T-Lymphocytes (T-Cells):** Produced in the bone marrow but migrate to mature in the **thymus**. They do not secrete antibodies directly but instead help B-cells produce them and mediate direct cellular attacks.
* **Humoral Immune Response (AMI):** Antibody-Mediated Immunity controlled by B-cells and plasma cells circulating in blood plasma and lymph (humors). It is highly effective against extracellular bacteria and viruses.
* **Cell-Mediated Immune Response (CMI):** Controlled entirely by T-lymphocytes (specifically Cytotoxic T-cells). It is the defense mechanism responsible for fighting intracellular pathogens, cancer cells, and mediating **graft rejection** during organ transplants.
* **Antibody Structure:** A glycoprotein molecule also called an **Immunoglobulin ($Ig$)**. It has an $H_2L_2$ structure made of **two heavy chains** and **two light chains** bound together by **disulfide bonds**.
* **Antigen-Binding Site:** Formed by the variable ($V$) regions of both heavy and light chains at the tips of the Y-shaped structure, known as the **paratope**. It binds specifically to the **epitope** of an antigen.
* **$IgG$:** The most abundant antibody in serum (~80%). It is the **only antibody capable of crossing the placenta** to provide passive immunity to the fetus.
* **$IgA$:** The second most abundant class, present predominantly in secretory fluids like saliva, tears, and **colostrum** (the yellowish initial milk secreted by mothers). It protects mucosal surfaces.
* **$IgM$:** The largest antibody (a pentamer held by a J-chain) and the **first antibody synthesized** in response to an initial antigen exposure.
* **$IgE$:** Involved primarily in **allergic reactions** and hypersensitivity. It binds heavily to mast cells and basophils, triggering histamine release. It also protects against parasitic helminth infections.
* **Active Immunity:** Developed when the host body is directly exposed to antigens (living or dead microbes) and synthesizes its own antibodies. It is slow to develop but long-lasting (e.g., natural infection or vaccination).
* **Passive Immunity:** Conferred when ready-made antibodies are directly injected or transferred into the body. It provides immediate relief but is short-lived and leaves no memory (e.g., anti-tetanus serum (ATS), anti-snake venom, or colostrum).

## Diagram in Words

Pathogen Entry -> Antigen Presentation -> T-Helper Cell Activation -> B-Cell Activation -> Plasma Cells -> Antibody Secretion ($IgG$/$IgM$) -> Antigen Clearance -> Memory Cell Formation -> Rapid Secondary Response on Re-exposure.

## Process and Function Focus

| Focus | What to Revise | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Humoral Immunity (AMI)** | B-cells differentiate into plasma cells to release circulating antibodies. | Effective against extracellular blood/lymph pathogens. |
| **Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI)** | Cytotoxic T-cells ($CD8^+$) kill infected host cells directly. | Primary culprit behind organ transplant graft rejection. |
| **Colostrum ($IgA$)** | Natural passive immunity transferred from mother to infant. | Protects the infant's respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts. |

## Quick MCQs

### MCQ 1

A patient undergoes a kidney transplant, but within a few weeks, the body rejects the foreign organ. Which component of the immune system is primarily responsible for this graft rejection?
A. B-lymphocytes and humoral antibodies
B. Cell-mediated immune response mediated by T-cells
C. Innate immune proteins like interferons
D. Mast cells releasing high amounts of histamine
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI), driven primarily by T-lymphocytes, is uniquely capable of distinguishing between "self" and "non-self" tissue components. It directly attacks and destroys foreign cells, making it the principal mediator of allograft rejection.

### MCQ 2

An individual is bitten by a venomous snake and is immediately rushed to the clinic where they receive an injection of anti-snake venom. What type of immunity does this treatment provide?
A. Artificial Active Immunity
B. Natural Passive Immunity
C. Artificial Passive Immunity
D. Natural Active Immunity
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Anti-snake venom contains pre-formed, ready-made antibodies collected from immunized animals (like horses). Because the patient's own body did not synthesize the antibodies, it is passive immunity; since it is administered medically, it is classified as artificial passive immunity.

### MCQ 3

Which of the following immunoglobulins is structurally configured as a pentamer and is the earliest class of antibody produced during a primary immune response?
A. $IgG$
B. $IgA$
C. $IgM$
D. $IgE$
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** $IgM$ is a heavy pentameric molecule consisting of five basic Y-shaped subunits linked together by a joining (J) chain. It possesses ten antigen-binding sites and is characteristically the very first antibody secreted following primary exposure to an antigen.

### MCQ 4

The specific molecular region located on the surface of an antigen that is recognized and bound by a corresponding antibody molecule is called a/an:
A. Paratope
B. Epitope
C. Idiotype
D. Constant domain
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The structural area on an antigen that interacts with the antibody is the epitope (or antigenic determinant). The matching area on the antibody tip that locks onto the epitope is called the paratope.

## Final 30-Second Revision Box

> 💡 **Ultimate Recall:** Acquired immunity relies on B-cells (humoral, antibody-producing) and T-cells (cell-mediated, graft-rejection). An antibody is an $H_2L_2$ glycoprotein held by disulfide bonds where the variable paratope binds the antigen's epitope. $IgG$ is the most abundant and crosses the placenta, $IgA$ guards secretions like breast colostrum, $IgM$ is a pentamer indicating new infections, and $IgE$ mediates allergies. Active immunity is slow but triggers memory, while passive immunity injects pre-formed antibodies for instant, temporary protection.
