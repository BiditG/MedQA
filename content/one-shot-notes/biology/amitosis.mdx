# Amitosis One Shot Note

## Concept in Simple Words

Amitosis, also called direct cell division, is the simplest way a cell splits. Unlike normal division (mitosis), the cell does not waste time condensing chromosomes, forming a spindle apparatus, or neatly aligning genetic material down the middle—the nucleus simply elongates, pinches in the center, and tears into two parts, followed by the rest of the cell body splitting.

## Why This Topic Matters in CEE

CEE questions on amitosis focus heavily on exceptions, specific occurrences, and differentiating it from mitosis and meiosis. Examiners look for the lack of spindle formation, chromatin condensation, and specific examples like mammalian cartilage cells or ciliate macronuclei.

## Must Remember Points

* **Discovery:** First described by **Robert Remak** (1855) in the red blood cells of chick embryos, though Flemming later coined the term "mitosis" for indirect division.
* **No Spindle Apparatus:** The most critical diagnostic feature of amitosis is the **complete absence of a spindle apparatus** and asters.
* **Nuclear Behavior:** The nucleus does not disappear; it directly elongates, develops a central constriction, and pinches into two daughter nuclei.
* **No Chromosome Condensation:** Chromatin material does not condense into distinct, visible chromosomes. Chromids or individual chromatids cannot be observed.
* **Unequal Distribution:** Since there is no alignment or spindle machinery, the genetic material is often distributed **unequally** between the two daughter cells.
* **Occurrences (Prokaryotes):** It is the standard method of division in bacteria and cyanobacteria (often generalized under binary fission).
* **Occurrences (Lower Eukaryotes):** Occurs during the division of the large **macronucleus** of *Paramecium* (which controls metabolic traits, while the micronucleus undergoes mitosis).
* **Occurrences (Higher Eukaryotes):** Found only in specialized, aged, or degenerating tissues in higher organisms, such as mammalian **cartilage cells**, foetal membranes (amnion), and endosperm cells of seeds.
* **Speed:** It is highly rapid compared to mitosis because it bypasses all complex structural stages (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase).
* **Result:** Produces two daughter cells that may not be genetically identical due to random, unequal nuclear cleavage.

## Diagram in Words

Normal Vegetative Cell -> Nuclear Elongation -> Dumbbell-shaped Nucleus -> Central Invagination of Plasma Membrane -> Direct Nuclear Cleavage -> Cytoplasmic Pinching -> Two Unequal Daughter Cells.

## Quick MCQs

### MCQ 1

Which of the following cellular events occurs during amitosis?
A. Condensation of chromatin into chromosomes
B. Formation of a spindle apparatus
C. Disappearance of the nuclear membrane
D. Elongation and constriction of the nucleus
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Amitosis is a direct division where the nuclear envelope remains intact throughout the process. The nucleus simply elongates and splits by a constriction into two parts without chromosome condensation or spindle assembly.

### MCQ 2

Amitosis is typically observed during the division of which of the following structures?
A. Micronucleus of *Paramecium*
B. Macronucleus of *Paramecium*
C. Human skin epidermal stem cells
D. Root apical meristem of *Allium cepa*
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** In *Paramecium*, dimorphic nuclei show different divisions: the large macronucleus (metabolic) splits directly via amitosis, whereas the reproductive micronucleus undergoes mitotic division.

### MCQ 3

Why can amitosis result in daughter cells that are genetically unequal?
A. DNA replication does not happen before division.
B. Spindle fibers pull whole sets of chromosomes to one side.
C. There is no random assortment or precise alignment of chromatin before pinching.
D. Mutations occur at an extremely high rate during the process.
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Because there is no spindle apparatus or structural alignment of chromosomes along a metaphase plate, the elongated nucleus pinches randomly, leading to an unequal volume of genetic material in each daughter cell.

### MCQ 4

In which of the following human tissues can amitotic cell division naturally occur?
A. Cartilage cells (chondrocytes)
B. Bone marrow hematopoietic stem cells
C. Zygote during early cleavage
D. Crypts of Lieberkühn epithelial cells
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** In higher mammals, amitosis is limited to specialized, old, or differentiating cells such as cartilage cells (chondrocytes) and the cells of embryonic membranes like the amnion.

## Final 30-Second Revision Box

> 💡 **Ultimate Recall:** Amitosis is direct division without visible chromosomes, nuclear membrane breakdown, or spindle fibers. Discovered by Remak, it results in unequal genetic distribution. It occurs routinely in bacterial fission and the *Paramecium* macronucleus, but is restricted to aging or highly differentiated tissues like mammalian cartilage or extra-embryonic membranes in higher organisms.
