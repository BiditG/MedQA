

# 90-Day CEE Preparation Plan: A Complete Roadmap for Serious Students

> **Only 90 days left for CEE?**
> Do not panic. Ninety days is enough to make a powerful improvement if you study with a clear plan, revise regularly, solve MCQs daily, and correct your mistakes honestly.

A 90-day plan is not about studying everything randomly.

It is about using your time wisely.

In 90 days, you need to:

* Complete important theory
* Revise high-yield chapters
* Practice MCQs daily
* Improve weak subjects
* Take mock tests
* Build speed and accuracy
* Stay mentally strong

The goal is simple:

> **Study smart. Practice daily. Revise repeatedly. Improve every week.**

This guide gives you a complete 90-day CEE preparation plan that is realistic, student-friendly, and focused on actual exam performance.

---

## Who Is This 90-Day Plan For?

This plan is useful for:

* Students who have already studied some chapters but lack revision
* Students who are starting serious preparation now
* Class 12 students balancing board and CEE preparation
* Gap-year students who need a structured plan
* Students who feel confused about what to study daily
* Students who have solved MCQs but are not improving
* Students who want a final 3-month strategy before CEE

If your basics are strong, this plan will help you revise and test yourself.

If your basics are weak, this plan will help you build structure and confidence.

---

## The Main Idea of the 90-Day Plan

The 90 days are divided into three phases:

| Phase   |      Days | Main Focus                                      |
| ------- | --------: | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Phase 1 |  Day 1–30 | Concept building and chapter completion         |
| Phase 2 | Day 31–60 | MCQ practice and revision strengthening         |
| Phase 3 | Day 61–90 | Mock tests, speed, accuracy, and final revision |

Each phase has a different purpose.

Do not treat all 90 days the same.

In the first month, you build.
In the second month, you strengthen.
In the third month, you perform.

---

## Phase 1: Days 1–30

# Concept Building and Syllabus Coverage

The first 30 days are for creating a strong base.

During this phase, your main aim is to complete important chapters and understand the core concepts.

Do not worry too much about full mock test scores in this phase.

Focus on learning properly.

---

## Goals for Days 1–30

By the end of the first month, you should:

* Complete high-yield chapters from all subjects
* Make short notes for revision
* Start solving topic-wise MCQs
* Identify weak chapters
* Build a daily study routine
* Start a mistake notebook
* Revise already studied topics weekly

> **Phase 1 Mindset**
> Do not rush. A chapter studied properly once is better than three chapters read carelessly.

---

## What to Study in Phase 1

### Physics

Focus on basic and scoring chapters first.

Suggested starting areas:

* Units and dimensions
* Vectors
* Kinematics
* Laws of motion
* Work, energy, and power
* Gravitation
* Heat and thermodynamics
* Waves
* Current electricity
* Modern physics basics

For every physics chapter, learn:

* Important formulas
* Units
* Graphs and relationships
* Common numerical types
* Conceptual traps

> **Physics Tip**
> Do not memorize formulas alone. Always solve at least 10–15 numerical MCQs after learning a formula.

---

### Chemistry

Divide chemistry into three parts.

### Physical Chemistry

Focus on:

* Mole concept
* Stoichiometry
* Atomic structure
* Chemical bonding
* States of matter
* Thermodynamics
* Chemical equilibrium
* Ionic equilibrium
* Electrochemistry
* Chemical kinetics

### Organic Chemistry

Focus on:

* Basic organic chemistry
* Isomerism
* Hydrocarbons
* Haloalkanes and haloarenes
* Alcohols and phenols
* Ethers
* Aldehydes and ketones
* Carboxylic acids and derivatives
* Amines
* Biomolecules

### Inorganic Chemistry

Focus on:

* Periodic table
* Periodic trends
* s-block elements
* p-block elements
* d-block basics
* Coordination compounds
* Important compounds and reactions

> **Chemistry Tip**
> Physical chemistry needs calculation. Organic chemistry needs logic. Inorganic chemistry needs repeated revision.

---

### Biology

Biology includes Botany and Zoology. It is usually high-scoring if revised well.

### Botany

Focus on:

* Cell biology
* Plant anatomy
* Plant physiology
* Photosynthesis
* Respiration
* Genetics
* Biotechnology
* Ecology
* Classification
* Reproduction in plants

### Zoology

Focus on:

* Animal tissue
* Human physiology
* Digestive system
* Respiratory system
* Circulatory system
* Nervous system
* Endocrine system
* Reproductive system
* Genetics
* Evolution
* Human diseases

> **Biology Tip**
> Biology should not be read like a story. Convert facts into questions and revise them actively.

---

### MAT

Mental Agility Test should be practiced regularly.

Focus on:

* Pattern recognition
* Figure series
* Number series
* Analogy
* Classification
* Coding-decoding
* Direction sense
* Blood relation
* Logical reasoning
* Basic arithmetic reasoning

> **MAT Tip**
> MAT improves through pattern exposure. Practice a little every day instead of leaving it for the last week.

---

## Daily Routine for Phase 1

A realistic daily plan can look like this:

| Time Block | Task                                  |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 1 hour     | Biology theory and recall             |
| 1 hour     | Chemistry concept study               |
| 1 hour     | Physics concept or numerical practice |
| 45 minutes | Topic-wise MCQs                       |
| 30 minutes | Mistake notebook                      |
| 30 minutes | Quick revision                        |

If you have more time, add more MCQs and revision.

If you have less time, reduce the number of topics, but do not skip MCQ practice.

---

## Weekly Target for Phase 1

Each week, try to complete:

* 2 Physics chapters or subchapters
* 2 Chemistry chapters or subchapters
* 3 Biology chapters or subchapters
* 3 MAT practice sessions
* 300–500 MCQs
* 1 mini test
* 1 weekly revision session

> **Important**
> In Phase 1, the number of chapters completed is important, but understanding and mistake correction are more important.

---

## Phase 1 Weekly Breakdown

## Week 1: Build the Routine

Main goal: Start studying consistently.

Tasks:

* Check the CEE syllabus
* Choose your main study materials
* Start one easy chapter from each subject
* Solve topic-wise MCQs
* Create a mistake notebook
* Practice MAT for 15 minutes daily

Recommended focus:

* Physics: Units, dimensions, vectors
* Chemistry: Mole concept, atomic structure
* Botany: Cell biology
* Zoology: Basic human physiology
* MAT: Pattern recognition

> **Week 1 Confidence Boost**
> Your first goal is not perfection. Your first goal is rhythm.

---

## Week 2: Strengthen Basic Concepts

Main goal: Improve conceptual clarity.

Tasks:

* Continue subject-wise study
* Practice more MCQs after every topic
* Revise Week 1 mistakes
* Start formula notebook
* Take one 50-question mini test

Recommended focus:

* Physics: Kinematics, laws of motion
* Chemistry: Chemical bonding, periodic trends
* Botany: Plant anatomy, plant physiology basics
* Zoology: Digestive system, respiratory system
* MAT: Number series, analogy

---

## Week 3: Increase MCQ Practice

Main goal: Move from reading to active solving.

Tasks:

* Solve MCQs daily
* Note repeated mistakes
* Start timed practice for easy topics
* Revise formulas and biology facts
* Take one subject-wise mini test

Recommended focus:

* Physics: Work, energy, power, gravitation
* Chemistry: Stoichiometry, states of matter
* Botany: Photosynthesis, respiration
* Zoology: Circulatory system, excretion
* MAT: Classification, coding-decoding

---

## Week 4: First Monthly Review

Main goal: Review what you studied in the first month.

Tasks:

* Revise all completed chapters
* Re-solve wrong MCQs
* Take two mini tests
* Identify weak subjects
* Adjust your next month’s plan

Recommended focus:

* Physics: Heat and thermodynamics
* Chemistry: Thermodynamics, equilibrium basics
* Botany: Genetics basics
* Zoology: Nervous system, endocrine system
* MAT: Mixed practice

> **End of Month 1 Checkpoint**
> By Day 30, you should know which subjects are improving and which subjects need more attention.

---

# Phase 2: Days 31–60

# Practice, Revision, and Weak Area Repair

The second month is where serious improvement begins.

In Phase 1, you learned chapters.

In Phase 2, you must test whether you actually remember and understand them.

This phase should include more MCQs, more revision, and more correction.

---

## Goals for Days 31–60

By the end of the second month, you should:

* Complete remaining important chapters
* Revise Phase 1 chapters
* Solve more mixed MCQs
* Improve weak areas
* Take regular mini tests
* Start full-length or half-length mock tests
* Improve speed and accuracy

> **Phase 2 Mindset**
> Your wrong answers are your teachers. Do not ignore them.

---

## What Changes in Phase 2?

In Phase 1, your ratio may be:

> 60% theory + 40% MCQs

In Phase 2, change it to:

> 40% theory + 60% MCQs and revision

This is important.

CEE is not only about how much you read. It is about how well you can answer questions under pressure.

---

## Daily Routine for Phase 2

| Time Block | Task                           |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1 hour     | Biology revision and MCQs      |
| 1 hour     | Chemistry practice             |
| 1 hour     | Physics numericals             |
| 1 hour     | Mixed MCQs                     |
| 30 minutes | Mistake notebook               |
| 30 minutes | Formula/reaction/fact revision |

Try to solve at least 80–120 MCQs per day in this phase if you are a full-time student.

If you have school or college, aim for 40–70 MCQs per day.

---

## Weekly Target for Phase 2

Each week, try to complete:

* 400–700 MCQs
* 2 mini tests
* 1 mixed subject test
* 1 full revision session
* 1 mistake notebook review
* 2 MAT practice sessions
* Remaining weak chapters

---

## Phase 2 Weekly Breakdown

## Week 5: Start Mixed Practice

Main goal: Move beyond topic-wise MCQs.

Tasks:

* Continue remaining chapters
* Start mixed MCQs from completed topics
* Revise Month 1 mistakes
* Take two mini tests
* Practice MAT twice this week

Recommended focus:

* Physics: Waves, sound, current electricity
* Chemistry: Chemical equilibrium, ionic equilibrium
* Botany: Biotechnology, ecology
* Zoology: Reproductive system, genetics
* MAT: Mixed reasoning

> **Week 5 Tip**
> Mixed MCQs feel harder because your brain must identify the topic first. This is exactly why they are useful.

---

## Week 6: Repair Weak Areas

Main goal: Fix chapters that are repeatedly causing mistakes.

Tasks:

* List your weakest 10 topics
* Study them again
* Solve MCQs from only weak topics
* Revise formulas and reactions
* Take one half-length test

Recommended focus:

* Physics: Electricity, magnetism basics
* Chemistry: Organic functional groups, isomerism
* Botany: Plant reproduction, genetics
* Zoology: Evolution, diseases
* MAT: Direction sense, blood relation

> **Weak Topic Rule**
> A weak topic becomes strong only when you revisit it, solve MCQs from it, and correct the exact mistake.

---

## Week 7: Increase Test Frequency

Main goal: Build exam temperament.

Tasks:

* Take 2–3 timed tests
* Analyze every test deeply
* Review silly mistakes
* Practice difficult MCQs
* Continue daily revision

Recommended focus:

* Physics: Optics, modern physics
* Chemistry: Electrochemistry, chemical kinetics
* Botany: Classification, ecology
* Zoology: Human diseases, immunity
* MAT: Figure series and pattern recognition

---

## Week 8: Second Monthly Review

Main goal: Check your actual progress.

Tasks:

* Revise all completed chapters
* Take one full or near-full mock test
* Review your accuracy subject-wise
* Identify top 5 recurring mistakes
* Prepare Phase 3 final revision list

At the end of Week 8, ask yourself:

* Which subject gives me the highest score?
* Which subject takes the most time?
* Which subject has the most silly mistakes?
* Which chapters still need revision?
* Am I improving in mock tests?

> **End of Month 2 Checkpoint**
> By Day 60, you should move from “I am studying” to “I am testing and improving.”

---

# Phase 3: Days 61–90

# Mock Tests, Final Revision, and Exam Readiness

The last 30 days are not for panic.

They are for performance.

You should now focus on:

* Full mock tests
* Speed management
* Final revision
* High-yield facts
* Formula recall
* Mistake correction
* Exam confidence

---

## Goals for Days 61–90

By the end of this phase, you should:

* Complete final revision of all major topics
* Attempt multiple mock tests
* Improve time management
* Reduce silly mistakes
* Strengthen weak areas
* Build confidence for exam day
* Prepare your final revision notebook

> **Phase 3 Mindset**
> Do not try to learn everything new in the final month. Strengthen what you already know and repair what is repeatedly costing marks.

---

## What Changes in Phase 3?

In Phase 3, your ratio should become:

> 20% new study + 80% revision, MCQs, and mock tests

This is the performance phase.

Reading new theory all day in the last month is usually not effective unless the topic is very important and completely untouched.

---

## Daily Routine for Phase 3

| Time Block | Task                                                   |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1–2 hours  | Mock test or timed MCQ practice                        |
| 1 hour     | Mock test analysis                                     |
| 1 hour     | Weak topic revision                                    |
| 1 hour     | Biology facts / chemistry reactions / physics formulas |
| 30 minutes | Mistake notebook                                       |
| 30 minutes | MAT or quick mixed practice                            |

Mock analysis must never be skipped.

Taking a test without analysis is like making a mistake and refusing to learn from it.

---

## Weekly Target for Phase 3

Each week, try to complete:

* 2 full mock tests
* 2–3 mini tests
* Daily revision
* Daily mistake correction
* 500–800 MCQs
* Formula and fact revision
* Time management practice

---

## Phase 3 Weekly Breakdown

## Week 9: Start Full Mock Practice

Main goal: Understand your exam-level performance.

Tasks:

* Take 1–2 full mock tests
* Analyze subject-wise score
* List weak chapters
* Review wrong answers
* Revise high-yield formulas and facts

After every mock, note:

* Total score
* Subject-wise score
* Number of silly mistakes
* Number of guessed answers
* Time taken
* Chapters needing revision

> **Mock Test Rule**
> Your mock score is not your identity. It is only feedback.

---

## Week 10: Accuracy Improvement Week

Main goal: Reduce careless mistakes.

Tasks:

* Practice slow and accurate solving
* Read every question carefully
* Focus on negative marking risk if applicable
* Avoid random guessing
* Revise confusing topics
* Take 2 timed tests

Common careless mistakes:

* Missing “not” or “except”
* Wrong unit conversion
* Formula substitution error
* Confusing similar biological terms
* Selecting answer too quickly
* Changing correct answers unnecessarily

> **Accuracy Tip**
> Speed is useful only when accuracy is stable. A fast wrong answer is still wrong.

---

## Week 11: Final Weak Topic Repair

Main goal: Fix the topics that still reduce your score.

Tasks:

* Choose 10–15 weak topics
* Revise them deeply
* Solve topic-wise MCQs
* Review old mistakes
* Take one full mock test
* Prepare final short notes

Do not try to repair 50 weak chapters at once.

Focus on the most important and most repeated mistakes.

---

## Week 12: Final Revision and Exam Confidence

Main goal: Keep your mind calm and your memory fresh.

Tasks:

* Revise formula notebook
* Revise mistake notebook
* Revise biology facts
* Revise organic reactions
* Revise inorganic trends
* Practice light MCQs
* Avoid learning too many new topics
* Sleep properly

> **Final Week Rule**
> The final week is for stability, not panic.

# Subject-Wise 90-Day Strategy

## Physics Strategy

Physics can be scary for many medical entrance students, but it becomes manageable with the right method.

### What to Do

* Learn formulas with meaning
* Practice numerical MCQs daily
* Revise units and dimensions
* Understand graphs and relationships
* Start with easier chapters
* Avoid skipping solved examples
* Keep a formula notebook

### What Not to Do

* Do not memorize formulas without practice
* Do not jump directly to hard numericals
* Do not ignore basic chapters
* Do not avoid physics completely
* Do not panic after one poor test

> **Physics Confidence Boost**
> You do not need to become a physics genius. You need enough clarity and practice to solve CEE-level questions accurately.

---

## Chemistry Strategy

Chemistry is a mixed subject. Some chapters need calculation, some need logic, and some need memory.

### What to Do

* Practice physical chemistry numericals
* Revise inorganic chemistry repeatedly
* Understand organic reactions through functional groups
* Make a reagent list
* Learn periodic trends
* Practice conversions
* Revise exceptions

### What Not to Do

* Do not read organic chemistry randomly
* Do not ignore reaction conditions
* Do not skip numerical practice
* Do not leave inorganic revision for the last day
* Do not memorize without solving MCQs

> **Chemistry Confidence Boost**
> Chemistry becomes easier when you divide it into physical, organic, and inorganic instead of treating it as one huge subject.

---

## Biology Strategy

Biology can give you strong marks if you revise consistently.

### What to Do

* Read actively
* Make question-answer notes
* Revise diagrams in words
* Memorize examples
* Practice MCQs after every topic
* Revise human physiology repeatedly
* Focus on diseases, functions, processes, and classifications

### What Not to Do

* Do not only read passively
* Do not ignore small facts
* Do not skip revision
* Do not confuse similar terms
* Do not assume biology is easy without practice

> **Biology Confidence Boost**
> Biology is not difficult because it is impossible. It feels difficult because it needs repetition.

---

## MAT Strategy

MAT is often ignored, but it can help improve your total score.

### What to Do

* Practice 15–20 questions daily
* Learn common patterns
* Solve figure series
* Practice number series
* Improve speed
* Review wrong reasoning
* Use timed practice

### What Not to Do

* Do not leave MAT for the final week
* Do not solve without checking logic
* Do not spend too much time on one puzzle
* Do not ignore visual patterns

> **MAT Confidence Boost**
> MAT improves with exposure. The more patterns you see, the faster your brain recognizes them.

# The 90-Day MCQ Practice Plan

MCQ practice should increase gradually.

| Days      | MCQ Type                | Daily Target |
| --------- | ----------------------- | -----------: |
| Day 1–30  | Topic-wise MCQs         |   40–80 MCQs |
| Day 31–60 | Topic-wise + mixed MCQs |  80–120 MCQs |
| Day 61–90 | Mixed MCQs + mock tests | 100–150 MCQs |

These numbers can be adjusted based on your schedule.

If you are in school or college, solve fewer MCQs but review them properly.

If you are a full-time gap-year student, aim higher but do not sacrifice analysis.

> **MCQ Rule**
> Solving 50 MCQs with proper analysis is better than solving 150 MCQs without learning anything from them.

---

# The 90-Day Revision Plan

Revision must be planned from Day 1.

Do not wait until the syllabus is complete.

Use this revision cycle:

| Revision Time | What to Do                            |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Same day      | Revise what you studied               |
| After 3 days  | Recheck key facts and formulas        |
| After 7 days  | Solve MCQs again                      |
| After 15 days | Review mistakes                       |
| Monthly       | Take mixed test and revise weak areas |

This method helps you remember information longer.

---

# Mistake Notebook System

Your mistake notebook should be your most important tool.

Use this format:

| Topic            | Mistake Type    | Correct Concept                                              | Action                  |
| ---------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Chemical Bonding | Concept mistake | Coordinate bond has shared electron pair donated by one atom | Revise Lewis structures |
| Human Physiology | Memory mistake  | Insulin is secreted by beta cells                            | Revise endocrine glands |
| Kinematics       | Formula mistake | Use v² = u² + 2as when time is not given                     | Practice 10 numericals  |
| Pattern Series   | Logic mistake   | Rotation was clockwise by 90°                                | Practice figure series  |

Review this notebook every week.

> **Mistake Notebook Truth**
> Repeated mistakes are more dangerous than difficult questions.

---

# Mock Test Strategy

Mock tests are very important in the final phase.

But mock tests should be used properly.

## Before the Mock Test

* Revise formulas
* Keep water nearby
* Sit in a quiet place
* Set a timer
* Avoid checking answers in between
* Treat it like the real exam

## During the Mock Test

* Read questions carefully
* Do easy questions first
* Avoid spending too long on one question
* Mark doubtful questions
* Do not guess blindly
* Keep track of time

## After the Mock Test

* Check your score
* Analyze subject-wise performance
* Identify silly mistakes
* Write weak topics
* Review wrong answers
* Revise related concepts

> **Mock Test Truth**
> A mock test is useful only if you analyze it. Without analysis, it is just another question paper.

---

# What to Do If You Fall Behind

Almost every student falls behind at some point.

That does not mean the plan has failed.

If you fall behind:

* Do not try to cover everything in one day
* Do not quit the plan completely
* Move unfinished low-priority topics to revision days
* Focus on high-yield chapters first
* Reduce perfectionism
* Continue MCQ practice
* Restart from the next day

> **Recovery Rule**
> Never destroy the whole plan because of one bad day.

A 90-day plan does not require 90 perfect days.

It requires returning to the plan again and again.

---

# What to Do on Low-Motivation Days

Some days you will not feel like studying.

On those days, do a minimum routine.

### Minimum Routine

* Revise 20 formulas or facts
* Solve 25 MCQs
* Correct 5 mistakes
* Read one short topic
* Plan tomorrow’s study

This keeps your rhythm alive.

> **Motivation Truth**
> You do not need full motivation every day. You need a minimum action plan for weak days.

---

# What to Avoid During These 90 Days

Avoid these mistakes:

* Changing books repeatedly
* Watching too many strategy videos
* Comparing with friends
* Ignoring weak subjects
* Studying only theory
* Solving MCQs without review
* Taking mock tests without analysis
* Sleeping too late every day
* Leaving revision for the final week
* Panicking after low mock scores

Your biggest enemy is not a difficult chapter.

Your biggest enemy is inconsistency.

---

# Final 10 Days Strategy

The final 10 days should be calm and focused.

Do:

* Revise formula notebook
* Revise mistake notebook
* Revise biology facts
* Revise organic reactions
* Revise inorganic trends
* Practice selected MCQs
* Take light mock tests if needed
* Sleep properly
* Keep confidence stable

Avoid:

* Starting too many new chapters
* Studying all night
* Comparing mock scores with friends
* Random guessing practice
* Panic scrolling
* Changing study materials
* Overloading your brain

> **Final 10 Days Reminder**
> Your job is not to create panic. Your job is to protect everything you have already studied.

You do not need to compare yourself with others.

Start today.

Follow the plan.

Protect your confidence.

And keep moving forward.
